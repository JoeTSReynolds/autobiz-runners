# Tribute (the person died recently)

A grey, slow, respectful edit of the person at their best, with two lines in their own voice as the spine and our closing line over the last shot. Emotional, never morbid: no cause of death, no hospital, no funeral footage, no news anchors.

- `spine`: exactly 2 lines the person says on camera, each a complete thought of at least 12 words, taken word for word from `transcripts` (copy the words exactly, start to end, no gaps). Choose lines that sound like a life philosophy, a thank-you, or what they cared about. Sincere beats funny. Each line must come from a shot where the person is clearly on camera.
  - Each line is about the person's own life, work, craft or beliefs, in the first person ("I", "my", "we"). Never a line where they remember, praise or mourn someone else (the viewer will think it is about them), and never an anecdote about another person.
  - Each line reads cleanly as a quote on a poster: no false starts, self-corrections or trailing asides ("but no, he has no, but..."), and no joke that needs its setup.
  - Prefer two different sources, but both lines from one source is fine; a speech, award acceptance or sit-down interview beats a chat-show bit.
- `moments`: 4 to 8 silent shots from at least 2 different videos (3 or more when the pool has them; one video looks like one clip) (the audio is muted under music) of the person smiling, laughing, performing, receiving applause, or at their best. Give the source id and the window in seconds (1.5 to 5 s); prefer close shots where only they are in frame (a guest beside them breaks the face crop). Use the transcript and its `[laughter]` tags to find them.
- `closingLine`: our own line, at most 14 words, about what they gave people (from their work or causes, nothing invented). No "RIP", no emoji.
- `name`: how audiences knew them (e.g. "Sir Patrick Stewart").

Order on screen: name card over moment 1, moments, spine 1, moments, spine 2, moments, closing card over the last moment, which should be a warm smile.
