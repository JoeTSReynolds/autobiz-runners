# Person edit

An intense edit about **what the person is talking about**, not a podcast clip: their story in their own words over an edit track (heavy bass, no vocals) that builds, drops, and then takes over completely. Every b-roll shot shows literally what is being said.

- If no transcript has the person telling the story in `pick.angle`, do not give up: tell the most gripping story the transcripts do contain in their own words (a near miss, a turning point, a record). The angle is why they are trending; the edit only needs a great story told by them.
- Interview and podcast captions have no speaker names. When the video is an interview with the person (their name in the title, e.g. "Joe Rogan Experience #1189 - Alex Honnold"), the long first-person answers ("I was…", "my…") are theirs and the short questions are the host's: use their answers.
- `source`: the one source the story comes from.
- `lines`: 6 to 12 sentences in story order, each copied exactly from `transcripts` (whole sentences, no gaps inside a sentence; you may skip sentences between lines). 25 to 38 seconds of speech in total. Start with the most gripping line (the hook), build tension, and end on a payoff line that lands hard.
- `dropLine`: the index of the line the music drops on (around 40% of the way in; the line that changes the story).
- `slams`: 2 or 3 stat slams that appear right after the drop line (at most 5 words, all caps, numbers welcome: "28 YEARS", "31,000 MILES ON FOOT").
- `popups`: 2 or 3 contrast pop-ups, short facts that sharpen a line (at most 8 words), each tied to a line index.
- `broll`: one literal search query per spoken idea (2 to 4 words, concrete nouns: "sea ice cracking", "gps map phone"), each tied to a line index, 6 to 12 queries.
- `footage`: 2 to 4 YouTube searches for real footage of the person in their best-known work or moments, the pictures a fan expects: film or TV scenes, performances, matches, award speeches ("The Graduate 1967 scene Dustin Hoffman", "Rain Man Dustin Hoffman scene"). Name the work and the person. Shots where their face shows are cut in during the speech and the final edit.
- `tail`: 6 to 10 queries for the pure edit after the payoff line, where speech stops and the music takes over: the most striking images of the story's world (places, objects, the person). Concrete and visual.
